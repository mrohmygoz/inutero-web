Coming home from journeys abroad to the warmth and love of family, "the voice of the free spirit" L8ching holds a listening party for new album "Chunzi." The album digitally releases 5/1, with the party held back home at the Beitou Hot Spring Museum as a sincere thank-you to the road that brought him here. His grandmother "Chunzi," the album's namesake muse, appears in person, and L8ching movingly presents her with flowers on stage: "Being your grandson is a lucky thing." Music legend Chen Ming-chang attends as a witness, with Sung Chu-lin, Dizang Voxx, and Tokyo saxophone master Motoharu lending support, showing off L8ching's broad network. He premieres the "I Love My Life" music video with director friend Birdy: "No script on the table, just whiskey." Stripping the arrangement back to simplicity, L8ching says: "I wanted to share, through music, the warmth and companionship my grandmother has given me."

!["Chunzi" album cover](/images/news/2026-05-01-l8ching-chunzi-listening-party/l8ching-chunzi-listening-party-cover.jpg "Chunzi album cover")

From field-recording Taiwan's mountain forests for *Dive & Give* to traveling to 10 recording locations across Southeast Asia for *South Expedition*, two-time Golden Melody Award nominee "the voice of the free spirit" L8ching held a media listening party yesterday (4/30) at the Beitou Hot Spring Museum for his third full original album, *Chunzi*, which also officially released digitally today (5/1) — a symbol of L8ching's journey home after traveling the world, offering thanks to the family whose love and care let him roam freely. The most moving moment of the event was L8ching's grandmother, "Chunzi" herself, showing up in person to offer her beloved grandson her heartfelt blessing. The album *Chunzi* is named after his grandmother, and sharing music with her in this space meant everything to L8ching, who emphasizes: "I think my grandmother is a genuinely simple person with a kind heart — even after seeing so much in life, she's kept her innocence and warmth, and I hope I can become someone like that too."

Beitou is where L8ching grew up, and also where his grandmother and album muse, Chunzi, lives. Choosing this century-old hot spring building as the venue for the listening party, L8ching explains: "It's closer to my grandmother's house, so she could bring friends from Beitou Park along too." The event stayed tightly focused on the theme of "coming home" — alongside inviting his old friend, Tainan native ayo, who's equally versed in old-time culture, to host, he also invited the mentor who discovered him back in the day — music legend Chen Ming-chang. Back then, L8ching and friends formed a band to enter the "Singing Facing South" Taiwanese-language songwriting competition, and were later signed to Chen Ming-chang Music Studio, joining Beitou's musical history and formally beginning his music career. On being able to invite Teacher Chen, L8ching says: "His dedication to the land has been a huge inspiration to me — it was through Teacher Chen that I first picked up the moon lute. He'd take us walking around Beitou, and that gave me a deeper connection to both the moon lute and my hometown. This time I used a lot of vintage gear and instruments, hoping to capture that same sense of time travel."

The listening party opened with L8ching performing "Breeze, Breeze" and "Blue Highway" live back to back, sketching out *Chunzi*'s dual character — a pentatonic vintage jazz nostalgia paired with the open-road feel of beach rock. He then walked through the album's origins: "In my past solo albums, my grandmother was always my most loyal listener. She'd play each album on repeat like a lullaby, humming along to the melody." But one time, video-calling her from abroad, L8ching noticed she kept listening to the same one Taiwanese-language song on repeat: "My earlier work leaned toward blues and R&B, which was less familiar territory for my grandmother — that made me want to write an album she could actually understand, and give it to her."

![L8ching presents flowers to his grandmother Chunzi](/images/news/2026-05-01-l8ching-chunzi-listening-party/l8ching-chunzi-listening-party-grandmother.jpg "L8ching presents flowers to his grandmother Chunzi")

To that end, L8ching stripped back the complex arrangements on this record, returning to a simple guitar-bass-drums structure, traveling to Dulan, Taitung, to seek out the back-to-basics philosophy of legendary producer Cheng Jie-ren, who produced Panai Kusui's *Ocean*. He also used vintage Showa-era microphones and samples from old family cassette tapes to build a retro feel, admitting: "Even though I wanted to make a simple album, it's so easy to keep wanting to dress it up more — I had to keep reminding myself that an album should capture real moments." L8ching adds, with feeling: "I think my grandmother is a genuinely simple person with a kind heart — even after seeing so much, she's kept her innocence and warmth, and I hope I can become someone like that too."

The event also premiered the music video for the album's lead single, "I Love My Life," with director and friend Birdy Nio — the go-to filmmaker for stars like Jolin Tsai and JJ Lin — showing up to support and share the deep bond behind the shoot: "We first met because we happened to be listening to a mutual friend's show and got to talking, and realized we were neighbors — and since playing hand-pan alone was boring, I invited him to drum with me, and we started making music together." L8ching adds: "The second day after meeting Birdy, he dragged me down to Hengchun — we set off driving at 1:30 in the morning and spent three days there making music," the two of them behaving like old friends who'd known each other for years. Birdy also shares that the concept behind the music video followed the song's title, "I Love My Life," itself — the team followed L8ching from Taipei to Hengchun, capturing his natural interactions with his musical collaborators: "We wanted to capture that feeling city people get — that even without truly going outdoors, writing and singing can feel like escaping the city." L8ching adds: "We had absolutely no script — just two bottles of whiskey on the table!" — and everyone ended up laughing together on Taipei's streets, a little tipsy, making music. Birdy also co-wrote the album track "Arrived in Paris" — back when L8ching failed an audition in Paris and vented to him about it, the two wrote the lyrics together over laughs. To recreate the legendary old sound of French records, L8ching even sought out a studio in Paris and recorded through a 1940s microphone to capture that continental texture, pouring his heart out in a foreign land.

L8ching's new work *Chunzi* was produced by L8ching himself alongside veteran recording engineer Hsu Chen-cheng, and the guest lineup shows off his wide circle of friends — beyond returning collaborators Wang Wei, Sujong Park, Emma, and Hong Guang-fu, the album also features a moving vocal performance from Sung Chu-lin, Ke-Le Chen's West African Asalato percussion, Dizang Voxx's powerful vocals, Hsu Pen Yi Yen's violin and harmonica, Hong Tzu-lung's piano, and saxophone from Tokyo jazz legend Motoharu, together composing a warm, heartfelt new chapter.

The listening party closed with L8ching performing the album's title track, "Chunzi." During the recording process, he repeatedly went home to spend time with his grandmother and share unfinished demos with her — every time she heard the closing lyric, "being your grandson is a lucky thing," Chunzi herself would break into a wide smile, which sparked an idea in L8ching: gathering the family's six grandchildren into a choir to sing their gratitude to her together, with Chunzi herself joining the harmony at the song's end, preserving that grandmother-grandson bond forever. After the song, L8ching walked off the stage to his grandmother's side and presented her with an early Mother's Day bouquet, bringing the audience nearly to tears, and encouraged the whole room to use the specially made "Chunzi" postcards on hand to tell their own mothers they love them, closing the event on a warm note. *Chunzi* is singer-songwriter L8ching's third solo album, released digitally worldwide on May 1, 2026.
