Dinner and a show! Music brand In Utero's wild collaboration with wildly popular hotpot chain Chan Chi — "In Utero Present Vol. 1: Hotpot Band Show" — wrapped up on 9/4. Fire EX. frontman Sam Yang, Panai, Elephant Gym, and Huan Huan all sang, giving the audience a double dose of sensory delight. Elephant Gym bassist Kaiting delivered her first show since a car-accident injury, and drummer Chia-Chin got a surprise birthday celebration that had the crowd cheering. Huan Huan, Mong Tong, and Chao Lin took the stage in turn, dazzling Chan Chi's regulars with their range of styles — and Sam Yang played an unplugged set, sharing a single guitar with Panai, his warm voice keeping the crowd company through dinner.

In Utero's very first self-produced event, "In Utero Present Vol. 1: Hotpot Band Show," took place yesterday (9/4) at the wildly popular hotpot restaurant Chan Chi Hot Pot's Ximen branch — the first-ever music event held there. The lineup included Panai, who just won Best Taiwanese-Language Album at the Golden Melody Awards; Fire EX. frontman Sam Yang; this year's Golden Melody Jury Award winners Elephant Gym; Huan Huan; Mong Tong; and Chao Lin, bringing together music and food for a combined feast. Audience members raved: "The sound in the private room was amazing — who knew a hotpot restaurant could work this well for a live show," "I was so close to the artists!", and "Dinner and a show together is such incredible value."

![Hotpot Band Show brought a combined feast of music and food](/images/news/2024-09-04-hotpot-band-show-wraps-up/hotpot-band-show-wraps-up-hero.jpg "Hotpot Band Show brought a combined feast of music and food")

Hotpot Band Show ran across three time slots yesterday. The first "pot" went to this year's Golden Melody Jury Award winners Elephant Gym, alongside companionship-minded band Huan Huan. It also marked Elephant Gym bassist Kaiting's first show since a car accident this past July — she shared an update on her recovery with the crowd, confirming she's now able to perform normally: "It still aches sometimes when I hit the injured spot, but I'm really happy to be part of something this special." Elephant Gym is also set to resume their injury-delayed "World" tour later this month, followed by a Taiwan tour in November including a headline show at Zepp New Taipei, with guests including 9m88 and Cosmos People frontman Xiaoyu. After closing a song, the event organizers — also Elephant Gym's Taiwan management team, In Utero — coordinated with the rest of the band to surprise drummer Chia-Chin, whose birthday had just passed: the whole room sang happy birthday as he blew out the candles on stage, turning 32 with fans by his side for an unforgettable birthday.

Huan Huan, up next, had already started eating before the show — vocalist Coco couldn't resist: "I can't help it, I already started eating, but I held back on the spicy part." Drummer Yi-zhen and bassist Shitou wore the limited "In Utero x Chan Chi" band tee, designed by renowned designer Lu Yi-hsuan of "Cypher Bio" (known for Golden Melody Award key visuals) and produced by streetwear label "Linshi Studio" — a crisp fabric with a streetwear cut that added a finishing touch to the event and reinforced Huan Huan's own "companionship band" charm. As their set wound down, Huan Huan also teased their upcoming show at Yucheng Theater in Nangang, Taipei on Saturday, September 7, sharing the results of their three-month, island-wide "Huan Huan Comes to You" project.

![Sam Yang performing, group photo](/images/news/2024-09-04-hotpot-band-show-wraps-up/hotpot-band-show-wraps-up-yang-dazheng.jpg "Sam Yang went unplugged, sharing a single guitar with Panai")

Psychedelic duo Mong Tong, past winners of the Golden Indie Music Jury Award, and Taiwanese-language vocalist Chao Lin performed in the afternoon slot — Hotpot Band Show was also Chao Lin's first proper full-scale performance. Chao Lin, a young artist from Kaohsiung, only started making music after the pandemic by chance, and has released three official singles so far, produced by veteran producer Li Yu-huan, known for his work with top-tier artists like Yvonne Yang, Valen Hsu, and Cheer Chen. On stage, he showed off wild charisma, even coming down to toast every table individually, channeling the retro flavor of an old-school "cabaret show." Audience members said: "That performance was incredible — you'd never guess it was his first real show," and "Every song had so much character, I can't wait for his debut album!"

Mong Tong, dubbed by the audience as the band best suited to performing at Chan Chi, blend traditional Taiwanese aesthetics with a distinctive masked stage presence, drawing raves like "Mong Tong is just built for performing somewhere this smoky" and "I thought they were part of the decor!" The uniquely styled Mong Tong will also hold their first-ever headline show later this month — 9/28 at Chenggong Temple in Taipei — featuring guest drummer Xiao Bai (of Deca Joins and a go-to drummer for artists like Enno Cheng and Chelsea Wang), plus guest appearances from rising Thai psychedelic rock act JPBS, presented alongside visual artist Huang Wei in the band's first large-scale production after more than three years of international touring.

The last act of the night was a pairing of Golden Melody artists — Fire EX. frontman Sam Yang opening, with Panai closing out the night. In a fun twist, Sam Yang had accidentally left his acoustic guitar at home in Kaohsiung, so he borrowed Panai's guitar on the spot — the two artists sharing one guitar felt completely natural, with the crowd saying "Sam Yang and Panai's guitar are such a good match, so cute." Sam Yang took the stage for a rare unplugged set, showing a side of himself completely different from Fire EX.'s punk-rock sound. He opened the night with a hit song, then generously took requests from the crowd, running through several more fan favorites — joking along the way: "Performing at dinnertime is kind of painful, because I kept smelling the hotpot the whole time — I'm starving!"

Panai took the stage next, opening with songs from the album that won her Best Taiwanese-Language Album last year, reciting a passage of the lyrics live. Midway through her set, she pulled the audience straight back into nostalgia with a couple of her older classics. She closed the night with a cover of a Hu Defu classic, bringing the event to a warm, melodic close — the perfect ending to In Utero's very first self-produced event, and leaving the audience excited to see what creative music projects the brand dreams up next.

Organized by In Utero Ltd.; in partnership with Chan Chi Hot Pot, Jiaoju Audio Co., Cypher Bio, and Linshi Studio.
