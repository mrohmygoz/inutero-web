SXSW 2026's Taiwan Beats Showcase expands its Day Party again, linking industry partners from multiple countries into an Asia-region matchmaking platform. The Showcase scores another win, bringing six acts to their US debut with bold, fresh sound and island spirit.

Fleshjuicer lands a headline feature on SXSW's own social channels, hailed by the festival as "a band you will absolutely never forget."

South by Southwest turned 40 this year with a fresh new format, and it wrapped with six Taiwanese acts — Enno Cheng, someshiit Sam, Fleshjuicer, Sorry Youth, Life of Movie, and Ounv Shan — taking the stage in relay at the Taiwan Beats Showcase on the evening of March 13, local time. Hosted by Taiwan's Bureau of Audiovisual and Music Industry Development, the Showcase kept up its momentum from past years at Austin's Venue 6, opening with a Day Party that afternoon linking Japan, Malaysia, and other Asian countries, and inviting industry professionals from the US, Canada, Czechia, Brazil, and beyond to connect with the six acts. "a-We," previously seen at Expo 2025 Osaka Kansai, showed up with a set of Taiwan-themed stickers for extra flair. The Day Party also served up bubble tea, salt-and-pepper fried chicken, braised pork rice, and spring rolls for a full cultural experience, drawing a steady crowd from afternoon into night.

![Taiwan Beats Showcase keeps its momentum going at Austin's Venue 6](/images/news/six-taiwanese-acts-sxsw-2026-recap/six-taiwanese-acts-sxsw-2026-recap-hero.jpg "Taiwan Beats Showcase keeps its momentum going at Austin's Venue 6")

This year's Taiwan Beats Showcase lineup drew attention from overseas media before it even took the stage — outlets including Rolling Stone sent photographers, 360 Degree Sound named Enno Cheng a standout artist of this year's SXSW and ran an in-depth pre-show interview introducing her to overseas listeners, Sorry Youth and Ounv Shan were both interviewed by Starry Constellation Magazine, and someshiit Sam and Fleshjuicer were invited to play additional official showcases. Ethan McConnell, music director at Austin radio station KTSW 89.9, recommended Fleshjuicer's live set in SXSW's official show guide, praising the band for pushing metal to its extreme while still folding in diverse styles and retaining Taiwanese traditional elements — and he showed up in person to watch. It's a clear sign Taiwanese artists continue to draw serious attention from international music media and curators around the world.

The evening's performances opened with someshiit Sam, backed by an all-star band — guitarist Lien Chen-tang, keyboardist Wang Ding-chung (DEW), drummer Xiao Bai, and DJ Flowstrong — whose full lineup warmed up the crowd instantly with a run of hits including "Those Who Told Me to Quit Smoking Are All Dead" and "Kindness Is," to fans' delighted surprise. Ounv Shan also played their full band lineup for their US debut, bringing several tracks from their new album *Hidden Album* and drawing attention with their distinctive electro-folk sound; the members told the crowd in fluent English how much they loved Austin's people, food, and weather, excitedly promising, "We're definitely writing a song for Austin when we get home!" Ounv Shan also specially reworked their signature track "Richard" into English lyrics for the overseas crowd.

![Fleshjuicer lands a headline feature on SXSW's own social channels, hailed as "a band you will absolutely never forget"](/images/news/six-taiwanese-acts-sxsw-2026-recap/six-taiwanese-acts-sxsw-2026-recap-meat-juice-machine.jpg "Fleshjuicer")

Life of Movie took the stage next with powerful rock energy, playing ten songs back to back — including "Is That Really Interesting," "I Often Have a Feeling," "I Want to Kill All the Romance, Kill the Poetry," and "Tell Me Where They Originally Were" — showcasing their singular live charisma and whipping the crowd into a roar. Fleshjuicer, one of this year's most-watched SXSW acts, packed the venue the moment they took the stage, drawing heavy media coverage as the crowd kept up circle pits and crowd surges through the set, pushing the energy to its peak. While playing "Birthday + Tiger God," a song steeped in Taiwanese folk tradition, Fleshjuicer shouted from the stage: "We are from Taiwan! Taiwan is a beautiful country worth loving — come visit us!" Before wrapping up, the band confidently told the crowd in English: "If you feel like time flew by, that means our show was really good!" The next day, Fleshjuicer landed a headline feature on SXSW's own social channels, hailed as "a band you will absolutely never forget," and the renowned punk and metal magazine Rock Sound ran a video feature recapping the night's performance.

Enno Cheng performed her English-language track "Passenger Side," sending a warm, romantic mood out into the Austin night for local fans. Making her first-ever US appearance, Enno Cheng said before the show she was thrilled beyond words to have the chance to perform there, thanking the magic of music for carrying her this far, and played several songs from her latest album *Still Moving*. On stage, she said with feeling: "I'm honored to be part of the Taiwan Beats Showcase." She also performed her English track "Passenger Side," sending that same warm, romantic mood into the Austin night for local fans. Sorry Youth, making their second SXSW appearance, closed out the night at midnight with songs like "Noise Apartment" and "Middle-Aged Embarrassment" that lit up the stage. Before playing "You Love Us Differently," they told the crowd in English: "We're so happy to play SXSW — this might be the festival that embraces the most diverse music, people, and cultural exchange anywhere in the world. Even where we're different, we all share the same love and passion for music." They closed the night with their classic track "Undercurrent," dedicated to Taiwanese people building a life in Texas, bringing the 2026 Taiwan Beats Showcase to a close amid cheers from the whole crowd.

This year, SXSW for the first time ran its music, film/TV, and tech-innovation programming simultaneously, opening up richer cross-disciplinary exchange and collaboration. The Taiwan Beats Showcase, long a flagship program at SXSW, fosters not only on-site exchange during the event but international matchmaking well before it, bringing in overseas advisors to share hands-on experience and helping participating artists connect more smoothly with the international industry. This year's Taiwanese performers didn't just win over crowds — they also built deeper connections with curators, labels, media, and musicians from around the world, laying groundwork for future overseas shows and collaborations and pushing Taiwanese music further onto the world stage.
