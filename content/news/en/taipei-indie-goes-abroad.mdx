For years, Taiwanese indie music circulated within a tight geography — venues in Da'an,
festivals in Taichung, the occasional Hong Kong booking. What's changed in the past 18 months
isn't the quality of the music. That was always there. What's changed is that the
infrastructure to move it has finally arrived.

Booking agents in Japan, South Korea, and parts of Europe have begun treating Taiwanese acts
the way they once treated Japanese acts in the early 2000s: as a source of genuine cultural
novelty rather than a novelty act. The distinction matters. Novelty wears off. Cultural
genuineness compounds.

In Utero has spent the past year building the connective tissue that makes this movement
durable — long-term relationships with trusted bookers, media contacts who understand the
context, and a roster of artists who know exactly who they are.

Three things had to line up at once:

- Bookers abroad who could read a Taiwanese lineup without a translator
- Venues willing to take a first-time act on a second-hand recommendation
- Artists prepared to tour on someone else's schedule
