From Taiwan to Austin: Taiwan Beats brings six acts to SXSW 2026 this March.

SXSW Music Festival (South by Southwest), which brings together musicians, industry professionals, creatives, and fans from around the world, turns 40 this year and runs March 12–18 in Austin, Texas. Six Taiwanese acts have been invited this year: Enno Cheng, who played her first major solo show at the Taipei Music Center last year; someshiit Sam, a hip-hop newcomer known for sharp personality and nuanced writing; Fleshjuicer, a metal band steadily expanding its international footprint; Life of Movie, famous for their live-show energy; Sorry Youth, twenty years into delivering Taiwanese-language rock straight to the heart; and Ounv Shan, who just released new work weaving urban feeling into electro-folk. Spanning rock, metal, pop, hip-hop, and electro-folk, the six acts bring a mature, wide-ranging take on what it means to reinterpret "Formosa" today.

![From Taiwan to Austin: Taiwan Beats brings six acts to SXSW 2026 this March](/images/news/six-taiwanese-acts-sxsw-2026-announcement/six-taiwanese-acts-sxsw-2026-announcement-hero.jpg "From Taiwan to Austin: Taiwan Beats brings six acts to SXSW 2026 this March")

Hosted by Taiwan's Bureau of Audiovisual and Music Industry Development and staged in Austin, "Taiwan Beats Showcase" continues to draw attention from international curators and critics for its ambitious lineups and programming. "Taiwan Beats" is the brand the Bureau built to bring Taiwanese pop music to global markets — reflecting the steadily rising production quality and diversifying scene of Taiwanese music in recent years, and introducing Taiwanese artists to audiences worldwide through the Showcase. All six acts make their US debut this year, bringing an open, unbound creative energy to show fans, labels, and media from around the world the many faces of a generation of Taiwanese music coming together. They don't confine themselves to any one stage identity, and they don't let audiences box them into a preset label — their work keeps opening new sensory possibilities for SXSW.

Enno Cheng, a same-year winner of both the Golden Melody and Golden Horse awards, expanded her musical range further last year with the album *Still Moving* — moving with ease from ballads to dance tracks, folktronica to alt-pop, and collaborating with creators from Hong Kong, Malaysia, Singapore, Japan, and Poland to give her work a cross-regional depth. This marks her Americas debut, and she hopes the stage will forge a deeper connection with overseas fans.

someshiit Sam, who won Best New Artist at the Golden Melody Awards and Best Hip-Hop Album at the Golden Indie Music Awards for his album *Yu Gong*, writes music like a memo to himself — putting life's contradictions, struggles, and subtle emotions into songs without disguise. His live show is built with several distinctively styled musicians from Taiwan's independent scene, forming an intensely charged alt-hip-hop narrative on stage.

![Enno Cheng, a same-year winner of both the Golden Melody and Golden Horse awards, released the album Still Moving last year in a striking new style](/images/news/six-taiwanese-acts-sxsw-2026-announcement/six-taiwanese-acts-sxsw-2026-announcement-hung-pei-yu.jpg "Enno Cheng")

Fleshjuicer, a Golden Melody-winning band, has been a frequent presence on international stages in recent years — from Korea's Busan International Rock Festival to Finland's F:F:F Festival, and an upcoming Japan tour — stunning audiences everywhere with a worldview that fuses Taiwanese-language lyrics with metal. This marks their first time on a US stage, and they promise an explosive new set along with word of a forthcoming album.

Sorry Youth, twenty years into their run, sculpt a new face for Taiwanese-language rock as a three-piece — songs like "Undercurrent" and "Brothers Should Have Dreams" repeatedly strike a chord with independent-music fans. Having previously taken part in SXSW through an online live session, they finally take the US stage in person this year, carrying the free spirit of Taiwanese-language rock further afield.

Life of Movie, who have repeatedly sold out venues, won Best Live Performance at last year's Golden Indie Music Awards on the strength of their live energy, and drew an enthusiastic response at Korea's Incheon Rock Festival. With a minimal but impactful musical vocabulary, poetic lyrics, and a detached stage presence, they've built their own rock narrative. This is also their first US show, and the band is looking forward to meeting international fans at SXSW.

Ounv Shan, who just released their new album *Hidden Album*, build a distinctive musical landscape out of electro-folk, leftfield pop, and a humanist point of view. Their signature track "Richard" has surpassed a million streams, moving between urban emotion and social themes with both tenderness and edge. Having expanded their international horizons through overseas shows and cross-genre collaborations in recent years, they bring a live set that's both pop-accessible and avant-garde, offering SXSW fans a fresh listening experience.

The Bureau of Audiovisual and Music Industry Development's active involvement in SXSW and its curation of "Taiwan Beats Showcase" are meant to make every US appearance more than a one-time fireworks display — building toward sustained growth in overseas markets. Through years of collaboration with SXSW's organizers, the Bureau balances genre range, gender balance, and market viability while retaining an active role in selecting acts. This year, the Bureau also made use of daytime hours before the evening shows to link up with Japan, Malaysia, and other Asian countries, co-hosting a Day Party and an Asia-region industry matchmaking platform for a more expansive, appealing model of cross-border collaboration.

Six Taiwanese acts head to SXSW in Austin with the Taiwan Beats Showcase — for more information, follow the Taiwan Beats Showcase website.
