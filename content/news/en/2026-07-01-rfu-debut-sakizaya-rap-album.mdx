R.fu, a blue-collar Indigenous artist on the Nanguaq label, spends his days unloading shipping containers alongside Indigenous coworkers under the sun; off the clock, he transforms into a rapper, singing soul-inflected rap woven through with the Sakizaya language. Today (7/1), he releases his debut mother-tongue rap album, "Carry THE WEIGHT" — using music to carry himself forward. It's not only Taiwan's first hip-hop album built around the Sakizaya language, but a "heavyweight" musical memoir weaving together contemporary Indigenous labor, the growing pains of urban life, and tribal faith.

![R.fu, "Carry THE WEIGHT" (illustrative photo)](/images/news/2026-07-01-rfu-debut-sakizaya-rap-album/2026-07-01-rfu-debut-sakizaya-rap-album-hero.jpg "R.fu, \"Carry THE WEIGHT\" (illustrative photo)")

R.fu says the album is dedicated to everyone who labors under the sun and everyone juggling music on the side: "I hope that beyond work, everyone can be brave enough to try more of what they really want to do, just like I did. We're people who carry everything on our shoulders — we carry our lives, and we carry the weight of our music too."

Of mixed Amis and Puyuma Indigenous heritage from Taitung, R.fu grew up in the urban Shulin district of New Taipei City. Through an unlikely encounter that crossed bloodlines, he connected with Sakizaya friends from Hualien's Malipuyo tribal community, who cared for him deeply and invited him to join their age-set system, giving him the tribal name "R.fu Laham" and his first set of traditional attire. Rather than approaching the Sakizaya language as a weighty mission of "saving tradition," R.fu simply leans into the language's own rhythm, rapping out his life story with the guidance of two language mentors.

"Carry THE WEIGHT" was produced by Howe Huang alongside mentor ABAO, pushing R.fu well outside his usual comfort zone, with top-tier arrangements from a Golden Melody-studded lineup including Musa Martin, Wang Yun-chu, Cheng Chieh, Hsu Ping, Ko Tsun-yu, and Li Wei-yung.

As his first "heavyweight" statement, the album's eight tracks dig deep into the life and faith of a new generation of working Indigenous youth. R.fu samples the clang of steel at the container yard, the clink of glasses at a drinking table, and tribal elders' muttering straight into the music, rendering working-class life with unapologetic presence. ABAO says, "This is an album with no ballads." Opening track "Zhengchuan" samples real container-yard sounds and features a surprise duet with Golden Melody rookie Makav, the two even tackling a Spanish-language verse together; the tender "I'll Follow You" layers up to 120 string tracks from Huang, Wang, and Musa Martin.

The Nanguaq family also pitches in, with Dremedreman Zengni, Kivi, Arase, and Brandy adding harmonies throughout — their warmth audible in every layer. ABAO can't stop praising her protégé's debut: "In his music, R.fu can be cool, obnoxious, full of swagger — but in daily life, he's just the most lovable, honest guy."

R.fu's debut mother-tongue rap album, "Carry THE WEIGHT," is now live on all major digital streaming platforms as of today (7/1) — an invitation to feel this year's album of sweat, tears, and the weight of faith.
