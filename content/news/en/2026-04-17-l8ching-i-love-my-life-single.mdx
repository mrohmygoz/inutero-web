Singer-songwriter L8ching releases new single "I Love My Life" today (4/17), built on a country-rock frame with banjo and organ layered in for a meadow-music atmosphere, inviting every listener to join him in saying "thank you" to every good moment life has offered. L8ching went to the ends of the earth for this single — traveling all the way to Dulan, Taitung, to learn from legendary producer Cheng Jie-ren, returning to his hometown of Beitou for the promotional photos, and calling in his old bandmates for a full-scale recording-studio session — every moment reminding him that he loves his life. This track is also the final pre-release single from L8ching's upcoming third solo album, *Chunzi*, out May 1. L8ching says: "I can't wait to share this chapter of my musical story with everyone!"

"I Love My Life" was born out of a specific writing exercise last spring, where L8ching deliberately made himself focus only on lyrics and melody — the moment an arrangement idea popped into his head or he started overthinking it, he'd switch straight to the next song and keep writing. That exercise flipped some kind of switch, producing over a dozen songs in one go, inspiration blooming like spring flowers. L8ching shares that naming the song took some deliberation too — the first working title was "Alive," and he also considered using the lyric "I love, I am" — but in the end, following *Chunzi*'s core principle of being simple and direct, he settled on "I Love My Life."

During recording, L8ching brought the album's songs to Lover Studio in Dulan, Taitung, to visit legendary producer Cheng Jie-ren, who produced Panai Kusui's *Ocean*, singing the songs to him one by one over pork and shaojiu cooked by engineer Old Wang. The two talked their way from music to the land, and the pace of Taitung genuinely slowed him down. That's when L8ching realized the Taipei-style production rhythm — six to eight hours of nonstop work like a weight-training session — simply wasn't right for this album. Soaking in the sea in the morning, climbing the hills to chat with friends at night, he found himself remembering, in that rhythm, what first inspired him to start releasing music: "A huge part of the inspiration came from the healing power of the east coast."

The most distinctive sound in the song comes from the banjo L8ching recorded himself. The idea traces back to college, when he first heard an unreleased Taylor Swift demo, "I Heart ?," and was moved by its toe-tapping banjo and the positive musical energy it carried. To shape the song's country-rock character, he borrowed a six-string banjo from musician Li Yong-en and spent a month or two getting familiar with it before recording a version he was happy with. L8ching says: "I know drumming and playing guitar carry a kind of direct joy, and I wanted to distill that same simplicity." For the recording session, he brought in old bandmates who've played on past works like *Dive & Give* and *South Expedition* — guitarist Wang Wei, go-to backing vocalist Emma Liu, bassist Hong Guang-fu, and percussionist Ke-Le Chen — recording a youthful, energetic drum take at Old Wang's studio and capturing friends' laughter, closing the song out with a group singalong that bundles up this gratitude and joy for the moment.

The promotional photos for the single and the *Chunzi* album were shot in the Beitou military dependents' village Zhongxin Xincun, a place L8ching used to pass through as a kid on family trips up the mountain to the hot springs. The village happened to be under renovation this year, which gave him the chance to shoot there. The faded green window frames reminded him of the small second-floor window at his old family home, and the old-style white curtains carried a gentle sense of time passing. L8ching's parents grew up in the village, and his grandmother is from Yunlin; after the shoot that day, he went to visit her, and she squinted at the photos and started telling old stories again. L8ching says: "I remember listening to *Chunzi* while we shot — I felt something really tender, and I nearly teared up."

L8ching, "the voice of the free spirit" and a double Golden Indie/Golden Melody-recognized artist, was nominated for multiple international awards in 2024 for "Pulso," a collaboration with Filipino sibling duo Ysanygo from the *South Expedition* project, and continues to venture across cultural borders. L8ching's new album *Chunzi* also arrives May 1 — he reveals the title "Chunzi" is taken from his grandmother's name, symbolizing both being born in spring and a self that's always sprouting outward and exploring. He hopes the new album can bring listeners back to simplicity and first intentions amid the noise, looking back toward the source that nourished them.
