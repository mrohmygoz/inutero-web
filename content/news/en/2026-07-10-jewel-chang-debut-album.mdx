Singer-songwriter Jewel Chang releases her debut album, "The New Past, The Old Still Here," on 7/10, four years in the making, with contributions from producer Chao Yu-chen along with Cheng Chao-yuan, Su Pei-ching, Everydaze, and Huan Huan's lead singer Coco. The album begins with a goodbye after her mother's death, tracing a path through denial, anger, longing, and regret toward making peace with life again — eleven songs, like eleven pages of a diary, piecing together a healing journey of her own.

![Jewel Chang, "The New Past, The Old Still Here" (illustrative photo)](/images/news/2026-07-10-jewel-chang-debut-album/2026-07-10-jewel-chang-debut-album-hero.jpg "Jewel Chang, \"The New Past, The Old Still Here\" (illustrative photo)")

Jewel's mother passed away suddenly in 2021, splitting her life into before and after — she once said it felt like she was stuck at twenty-five, the age she was when her mother left. She expected time to carry the grief away, but what actually changed was how she came to understand the past. "New life keeps happening, but the old never really leaves." "The New Past, The Old Still Here" grew out of that realization.

The album brought together many musical collaborators, produced by Chao Yu-chen with Cheng Chao-yuan, Chiang Hsi-yi, Kuo Yi-che, and Everydaze's Wang Yu-sheng on arrangement and production — keeping folk's narrative core while adding harp, strings, and mandolin alongside more contemporary sonic experiments, expanding what folk music can express.

After her mother's passing, Jewel also began rethinking what it means, as a woman, to grow up, learn to protect herself, and become a true "adult." She invited women who shaped her artistically to join her — Su Pei-ching, whose harp and harmonies feel like a conversation across time, and Huan Huan's Coco, whose delicate arrangement and sound design helped Jewel approach feelings she'd once been afraid to touch.

Beyond eight fully produced tracks, the album keeps three demos intact, preserving the rawest version of Jewel writing and singing alone in her room. It also marks the first time she recorded her own guitar in a formal session — she was so nervous she nearly forgot to breathe.

The album's eleven tracks form a complete healing journey. It opens with "Bali," learning to breathe again on a trip taken to escape; "This Moment" is the first admission that "nothing can go back to how it was"; "February" looks squarely at her deepest connection with her mother. On the closing track, "Gently Laid to Rest," Jewel finally writes that she's arrived at the next chapter: even after this hard passage, given the choice again, she'd still choose to be her mother's child.

Jewel Chang's debut album, "The New Past, The Old Still Here," is officially out 7/10, with the music video for lead single "Gently Laid to Rest" following on 7/15. Jewel says the album is a letter left for the past, and written for the future.
