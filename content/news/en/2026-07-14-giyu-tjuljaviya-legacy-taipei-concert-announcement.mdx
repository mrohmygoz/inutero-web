Giyu Tjuljaviya, widely regarded as the biggest dark horse at the 36th Golden Melody Awards after winning Best Lyricist, officially announces his first large-scale solo concert — "Giyu Tjuljaviya: Like Cloud's Shadow Follows, 2026 Taipei Concert" — on Saturday, August 22, at Legacy Taipei.

![Giyu Tjuljaviya: Like Cloud's Shadow Follows, 2026 Taipei Concert (illustrative photo)](/images/news/2026-07-14-giyu-tjuljaviya-legacy-taipei-concert-announcement/2026-07-14-giyu-tjuljaviya-legacy-taipei-concert-announcement-hero.jpg "Giyu Tjuljaviya: Like Cloud's Shadow Follows, 2026 Taipei Concert (illustrative photo)")

This show is the final piece of the experience built around his album "Clouds Will Cross the Mountain," expanding the album's aesthetic of "scenery you can hear." Drawing on over a decade of songwriting, the concert guides the audience through different eras of his creative life via sound landscapes, immersive visuals, and stage design. A specially crafted physical installation will let the audience interact directly, taking home a piece of the show.

As an urban Indigenous person without tribal upbringing, Giyu Tjuljaviya turns his own anxiety over an unfamiliar mother tongue and an identity rooted only in bloodline into creative fuel, writing songs about local memory, family history, and the struggle for belonging through everyday, literary language and folk music in his native tongue. The central image, "cloud's shadow," carries deep meaning: even when two shadows overlap, an unbridgeable distance remains — a metaphor for how the public's understanding of Indigenous people often stays at the surface, like trying to understand a cloud only through its shadow. All of it ultimately resolves into the show's Paiwan-language title, mahepaljaung — "sheltering and looking out for one another."

The show carries deep thought in both its visual and sonic craft. The key visual is by Huang Chia-hung, a Golden Melody Best Album Design nominee, inspired by classical photographic "developing" techniques to evoke fragments of the past slowly coming into focus. Visual artist Wang Tuo handles VJ design on stage, while music director Hung Tzu-lung built an entirely new arrangement for the show, backed by a top-tier band: drummer Lei Ching, guitarist Kuo Yi-che, bassist Gao Chao, and keyboardist Smario.

For the show's accompanying film series, the team selected the most resonant tracks from "Clouds Will Cross the Mountain" for visual treatment — Giyu cast his own aunt as the lead, and personally returned to Dawu tribal village to visit its sole culture-bearer, an unexpected visit that turned into a fantastical reunion with long-lost family.

"Giyu Tjuljaviya: Like Cloud's Shadow Follows, 2026 Taipei Concert" is now on sale.
