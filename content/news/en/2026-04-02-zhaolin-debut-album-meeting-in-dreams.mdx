Taiwanese-language rising star Zhaolin releases debut album "Meeting in Dreams," following a warmly received premiere at Megaport Festival last month. Five years in the making, produced by veteran producer Lee Yu-huan, with contributions from writers Wang Chao-hua and Chang Ya-chun. Zhaolin also releases a second music video with rising Hong Kong animator Greg for lead single "Wind," and promises a companion album next month.

![Zhaolin "Meeting in Dreams" digital cover](/images/news/2026-04-02-zhaolin-debut-album-meeting-in-dreams/zhaolin-debut-album-meeting-in-dreams-cover.jpg "Zhaolin Meeting in Dreams digital cover")

"The voice of infatuation," Zhaolin, officially released his debut solo album "Meeting in Dreams" last month (March), produced by Lee Yu-huan — a musician who has worked with artists including Sandee Chan, Sandy Lam, Yolanda Cheng, and Sammi Cheng — pairing electronic-music foundations with a distinctly styled take on Taiwanese-language songwriting. His first live show since the release came late last month at Megaport Festival, where he debuted the new material with his full "Zhaolin & Sweet Kiss Kiss" band lineup, drawing a crowd of dedicated independent-music fans who lined up outside well before the show and packed the venue. Performing tracks from the new album including "Wind," "Longing on the Cotton Bed," and "Moonlight Shadow," he won over the full house, with fans calling it "incredible."

Since 2024, Zhaolin has steadily released singles built on an electronic-music foundation with a distinctly personal take on Taiwanese-language songwriting, earning a loyal following and drawing attention from the scene — recommended by writer Hsiao Yi-hui, "Taiwan's #1 Commuter Brand" personality He A, and musician cia cia Ho Sin-Ting. Zhaolin reveals that "Meeting in Dreams" was actually meant to come out two years ago, but kept getting pushed back through endless back-and-forth in post-production — counting from the start, it took nearly five years to finish. Zhaolin shares: "These songs were originally locked away in my room — I just wanted to make something for myself to listen to, and somehow that turned into a five-year road. This unexpected delay actually became a process of re-sorting my whole worldview. I went through countless cycles of dying and coming back to life along the way — every time I touched the core of it, life would tear everything down again and force me to look at myself anew from the ashes."

The title "Meeting in Dreams" was decided early in the creative process. Zhaolin recalls: "Back then I was living a life of working by day and arranging music at midnight. Afterward, exhausted, I'd fall asleep with the demo I'd just finished that night still in my head. During that period, the demos in my headphones would blend into my dreams, producing all kinds of fantastical, battle-royale-like scenes. That state of extending real-life struggle into dreams became the tone of this album." He recently released the music video for lead single "Wind," continuing a connection with Hong Kong animator Greg that started last year with "ima wa ima" — their second collaboration, still working with an instant, easy chemistry. Zhaolin says: "My collaboration with Greg has always existed in a state that's hard to put precisely into words. There's a very intuitive connection between us — a lot of the time, we don't really break the work down rationally, we just let the work itself do the talking."

![Full "Zhaolin & Sweet Kiss Kiss" lineup debuts the new songs live, drawing a packed, supportive crowd](/images/news/2026-04-02-zhaolin-debut-album-meeting-in-dreams/zhaolin-debut-album-meeting-in-dreams-portrait.jpg "Zhaolin")

During the making of his debut album, alongside the 12 tracks it contains, Zhaolin was also writing a number of other new songs and formed the "Zhaolin & Sweet Kiss Kiss" band with musician friends for live shows and further co-writing. Zhaolin gives an early preview: "'Meeting in Dreams' is the first chapter (CD1) of a two-part album — next month I'm releasing the second full album (CD2). The songwriting and production approach are completely different, but there are some thematic threads that mirror and reflect each other — stay tuned!" — a striking display of creative output.

For "Meeting in Dreams," Zhaolin brought in writer and singer-songwriter Wang Chao-hua and Taiwanese-language songwriter Chang Ya-chun to help polish the songs and bring out the distinct flavor of Taiwanese-language lyricism. The album's mixing and mastering were handled by producer Adrian Chan, who has produced albums for Anthony Wong, at17, and Denise Ho, drawing out the sensory collisions found in dreams from the writing stage through to a post-production push toward a dreamcore-like, hazy texture — the album was also released in a Dolby Atmos mix for an immersive listening experience.

Kaohsiung native Zhaolin was also invited to perform at his hometown's major festival, Megaport, late last month (March), drawing a full house of supporters. Despite having performed in various settings over the past two years, he still felt a particular hometown nervousness, sharing: "Megaport, to me, felt like a formal ceremony of meeting everyone. Performing near home and looking out at the audience gave me a surreal feeling — those private dreams that had only ever existed in my headphones suddenly became real in a familiar space back home. Seeing old neighbors from Yancheng mixed in with new friends in the same crowd turned all that nervousness into a feeling of coming home." Zhaolin also hopes to get better at managing his stage energy going forward, to enjoy each moment even more.

This month, Zhaolin will perform alongside writer Wang Chao-hua at the Du Shu Festival, with more chances to meet fans live ahead. For more on Zhaolin, follow his Facebook page and Instagram.
