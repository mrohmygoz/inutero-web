Public Television Taigi's annual music initiative "2026 Our Song: Taiwanese Children's Songwriting Open Call" has been underway for several days. Judging chair Chen Jianwei recently appeared on programs including "Formosa Boys" and "Dalun's Big Heart" to talk about the initiative, openly sharing tips for writing children's songs — including the story of how his daughter, in a fit of frustration over homework, wrote "100 Points Is a Piece of Cake." He's encouraging anyone with a passion for music to submit boldly, with a chance to have a favorite judge help finish the song.

**Breaking from "educational" children's songs, letting kids' logic run wild**

On the shows, Chen Jianwei discussed what today's kids actually respond to in music, noting that children's songs were once expected to carry a strong "educational purpose," but today's kids gravitate toward music that's simply entertaining. He pointed to old-school nonsense rhymes with zero logical structure that kids found endlessly fun and that have stuck around for generations. In his view, switching from an adult perspective to a kid's-eye view massively widens what's possible in Taiwanese-language children's songwriting.

**Chen reveals his daughter's song, "100 Points Is a Piece of Cake"**

Chen says kids have boundless creative potential and a unique point of view. His daughter, having absorbed his own creative process growing up, had asked more than once to write her own song. Recently, frustrated by exam pressure, she channeled that anger into a song — Chen rearranged it and had her rap a verse — and the result, a playfully deadpan track called "100 Points Is a Piece of Cake," was a hit online as soon as it was posted.

![Chen Jianwei shares his daughter's self-written song "100 Points Is a Piece of Cake" on air (Image courtesy of Microcosm Music Studio)](/images/news/2026-06-17-chen-jianwei-daughter-song-contest/2026-06-17-chen-jianwei-daughter-song-contest-daughter-song.jpg "Chen Jianwei shares his daughter's self-written song \"100 Points Is a Piece of Cake\" on air (Image courtesy of Microcosm Music Studio)")

Chen also addressed the music industry's growing focus on AI-generated creation, saying bluntly that the judging panel can always tell the difference between an AI voice and a real instrument. His advice for creators: AI is a great tool for logging and organizing ideas, but it should never fully replace singing or personal emotional investment — a work still needs to keep its own soul and character.

**A dream team of collaborators — "2026 Our Song" is open for submissions through 7/5**

"2026 Our Song: Taiwanese Children's Songwriting Open Call" has assembled an unprecedented panel of Taiwanese-language songwriting masters, including Bulao, Wang Chao-hua, Leo Wang, Howe Huang, and Michelle Hsiao. Selected demos earn a NT$100,000 prize, and entrants can also name a favorite songwriter to help produce the finished track — Public Television Taigi will turn every selected song into a polished animated music video for broadcast. Each entrant may submit up to 3 works to improve their odds. Full rules and guidelines are available on Public Television Taigi's official event site.
